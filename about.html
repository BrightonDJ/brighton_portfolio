About

From aerial imagery to agentic systems.

A year into research, one clear direction: build systems that reason, not just recognize.

I started in computer vision — oriented object detection for aerial and remote sensing imagery, the kind of problem where a bounding box has to rotate with the object instead of just boxing it in. That work taught me how to take a research idea and turn it into something that actually runs, gets measured, and produces measurable results.

Somewhere in the middle of that, I ended up leading. Not by plan — a platform needed building, a team needed direction, and I was the one who understood both the research and the engineering well enough to hold it together. I've now led two five-person R&D teams: one building an agentic RAG platform for enterprise data, another building TrainForge AI, a platform to train and deploy detection models end-to-end. My work has expanded into enterprise AI, where I've been architecting RAG-based systems for multi-format enterprise data and exploring agentic orchestration.

Building intelligent systems across computer vision, enterprise AI, and agentic systems — GenAI isn't a departure from what I've been doing, it's an expansion of it. The same instinct that made oriented detection interesting to me (systems that model something more precisely than the obvious approach) is what pulls me toward agents: systems that don't just retrieve or classify, but plan, reason, and act.

Research Philosophy

I approach AI research by combining mathematical understanding, empirical experimentation, and engineering practice. Rather than treating models as black boxes, I focus on understanding their underlying mechanisms, evaluating their behavior experimentally, and translating research insights into practical systems.
