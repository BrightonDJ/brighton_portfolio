About

From mathematical modeling to intelligent AI systems.

Researching, building, and evaluating AI systems across computer vision, enterprise AI, and agentic systems.

I started in computer vision, particulary oriented object detection for aerial and remote sensing imagery, the kind of problem where a bounding box has to rotate with the object instead of just boxing it in. That work taught me how to take a research idea and turn it into something that actually runs, gets measured, and produces measurable results.

Somewhere in the middle of that, I ended up leading. I've now led two five-person R&D teams: one building an agentic RAG platform for enterprise data, another building TrainForge AI, a platform to train and deploy detection models end-to-end. My work has expanded into enterprise AI, where I've been architecting RAG-based systems for multi-format enterprise data and exploring agentic orchestration.

GenAI feels like a continuation of that work more than a new direction. The same interest in systems that model things precisely instead of taking the obvious shortcut is what draws me to agents now: systems that plan and reason instead of just retrieving or classifying.

Research Philosophy

I approach AI research by combining mathematical understanding, empirical experimentation, and engineering practice. Rather than treating models as black boxes, I focus on understanding their underlying mechanisms, evaluating their behavior experimentally, and translating research insights into practical systems.

Mathematics → AI

A PhD in Mathematics shapes how I approach AI research — not as a separate qualification, but as the foundation underneath it. Each stage below fed directly into the next.

01
Mathematical Modeling
PhD research in mathematical modeling and scientific computing. The discipline of representing a real problem precisely before trying to solve it.
02
Optimization
The mathematics behind how every model in this portfolio actually learns: gradient descent, loss formulation, and the trade-offs in between.

Geometry
Rotation, coordinate transforms, and rotated IoU. The geometric core of oriented object detection, worked through in detail on the Research page.

Computer Vision → AI Systems
Oriented object detection, agentic RAG, and TrainForge AI. Mathematics and deep learning applied to systems people actually use.
